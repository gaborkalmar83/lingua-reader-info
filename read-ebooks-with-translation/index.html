Read ebooks with translation on Android

A book in the language you are learning is good practice, until the first sentence you cannot follow stops you. This guide shows how to read an EPUB or PDF on Android with the translation under every sentence, using Bilingua Reader, and what to expect from each way of translating.

What you need

  • A book as an EPUB or PDF without DRM. It can be a file on your phone or a free book from an online catalogue: the app can browse OPDS catalogues such as Project Gutenberg, calibre, Calibre-Web, Komga and Kavita.
  • Two limits to know first: books protected by DRM cannot be opened, and a scanned PDF without a text layer cannot be translated, because the app has no OCR.

Step by step

  1. Install the app and look at a starter book. Two short starter books and an in-app guide are included, so you can see translations working within a minute.
  2. Download the offline language packs. The Languages screen lists the 59 supported languages. Each pack is downloaded once, on Wi-Fi only by default, and after that translation works without a connection.
  3. Add your book. Use the plus button in the library to pick a file from your phone or a book from a catalogue.
  4. Open it and read. Sentences are translated as they come into view, and the translation appears directly under each sentence.
  5. Look up a word when you need to. Long-press a word to see its meaning, the sentence it came from and that sentence translated. Double-tap a word to save it for later.

Choose a layout

The translation can sit on its own line under the sentence, which is the default, on the same line in quotes, or be switched off. There is also a hide option that blurs one side until you tap it, as a nudge to recall the meaning yourself before you check. Pick the layout that lets you read the original first and glance down only when you need to.

EPUB and PDF are handled differently

An EPUB is shown as its publisher made it. A PDF is re-flowed into readable text so that sentence translation works there too. The price is that a PDF loses its layout, figures, tables and page images. If the layout matters to you, an EPUB of the same book is the better choice.

Offline or online translation

By default translation runs on your phone, using on-device models. Nothing you read is sent anywhere, and it works on a plane. The honest trade-off is quality: on-device translation is good for understanding but not for the finer points of nuance, and for a pair of languages that does not include English it is weaker, because the models translate through English internally.

If you want more fluent output, you can switch to an online provider in Settings: Google Translate, DeepL, MyMemory, Azure AI Foundry, OpenRouter or your own LibreTranslate server. Some of these need your own key. The app shows a notice when you switch, because from then on the sentence being translated is sent from your phone to that provider over the internet, and the provider sees your internet address like any web service would. Every translation is stored per book, so a book is translated once and reopening it does not send the same sentences again.

Reading on the go and at night

Because the packs and the translations are stored on your phone, a book you have already opened keeps working without a connection. The reader has light and dark themes, and page themes where you choose your own text, background and translation colours, which helps when you read in the evening.

Before you start a long book

Try the starter book first, then a short chapter of your own book, and check that the layout and the translation quality suit you. Switching provider or layout later is a setting, not a new import.

Questions and answers

Does reading with translation work without internet?

Yes. Translation runs on your phone by default, using language packs that you download once. After that it works without a connection. Online providers are optional.

Can I read a PDF with the translation under each sentence?

Yes, as long as the PDF has a text layer. The app re-flows the text so that sentence translation works, which means the original layout and figures are not kept. A scanned PDF without a text layer cannot be translated, because there is no OCR.

What does an online translation provider receive?

Only if you switch to one: the sentence being translated is sent from your phone straight to that provider, together with the key you supplied where one is needed. The app tells you this when you switch. With the default offline translation nothing you read leaves your phone.
